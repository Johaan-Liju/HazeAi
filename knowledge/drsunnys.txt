They serve as a root for a crown, a type of prosthetic tooth. Unlike dentures and bridges, dental implants offer a permanent and long-term solution. They provide a natural look, prevent bone loss, and improve your smile.
There are various types of dental implants available and based on your condition, your dentist will suggest one. Endosteal, Subperiosteal, All-on-4 Implants, Zygomatic and Mini Dental implants are the different kinds of dental implants used. Consulting a doctor can help you determine the right implant option for you. Let's look at the types of dental implants
Understanding the Different Types of Dental Implants
Endosteal Implants
Endosteal dental implants are the most common type of dental implants which are placed in the jawbone. They are made from titanium and are in the shape of small screws. These implants are recommended for patients with strong jaw bones. Known for its stability and longevity, endosteal dental implants give you a natural look and feel.
Subperiosteal Implants
Subperiosteal dental implants are placed on or above your jawbone. These are apt for patients who do not have a healthy jaw and are unwilling to undergo bone grafting. A custom metal frame is placed over the jaw bone and waits for the gums to surround it. Later, artificial teeth are attached to the frame which extends through the gums. This implant is suitable for patients with bone loss and requires a short treatment time. You have to take good care as there are high chances of infection and they are not stable as endosteal implants.
All-on-4 Implants
This treatment method is suggested by doctors for patients who need a full set of teeth either top or bottom. Four implants are placed in each jaw, two in the front and two in the back, to increase contact with the bones. Then a temporary prosthetic arch is attached to the implants and left to heal. After 3-6 months a permanent custom prosthetic arch is attached to the implant. This method is suitable for patients who miss multiple teeth. These are cost effective and require only a shorter healing time than other treatments.
Zygomatic Implants
Zygomatic implants are the least common type and are placed on cheek bones instead of jaw bones. These are recommended for patients with significant bone loss and who can't undergo traditional implants. Additionally there is no need for bone grafting which takes more time to heal. This is an efficient tooth replacement process with a high success rate. Zygomatic implants require long procedure time and high cost.
Mini Dental Implants
Mini dental implants are half the size of regular implants, requiring less bone density. These implants can be placed without grafting and are a less invasive option for patients who may not have enough bone for standard implants. Due to its small size, it is easier to place and has a quicker recovery period. They are more affordable but not as durable and stable as other implants.
Benefits of Dental Implants
The benefits of dental implants are several. Dental implants are durable and have a high success rate. They prevent bone loss that happens after you lose a tooth. Dental implants serve as an artificial root for your teeth. The natural look of the teeth is achieved by placing a crown over the implant. A dental implant not only looks like a natural tooth but also functions like one. While you are missing a tooth, the adjacent tooth can move towards the gap and create problems with your ability to chew. Replacing a lost tooth can bring back your smile and help you to regain your lost confidence. Overall, dental implants can improve your quality of life.

What is Gum Disease?
Gum disease is a bacterial infection of the gums brought on by plaque accumulation. Plaque is a sticky, colourless layer of bacteria formed on the surface of the teeth. It can be regarded as the major gum disease symptom. If it isn't removed by daily brushing and flossing, plaque turns to tartar, which can only be eliminated by a dentist or hygienist. Tooth loss and other major health problems may result from the illness, which severely impacts not just the gums but also the bones that support the teeth.
Stages of Gum Disease
Stage 1: Gingivitis
Gingivitis is the early stage of gum disease, fortunately, it is curable with the right care. Particularly while brushing or flossing, the gums may appear red, swollen, and prone to bleeding at this time. Plaque deposition around the gum line is frequently the cause of gingivitis. Gingivitis, however, can be prevented before it spreads if it is treated with proper dental hygiene techniques including brushing twice a day, flossing, and cleaning your teeth from dental experts.
Stage 2: Periodontitis
Gingivitis can develop into periodontitis, a more severe form of gum disease, if the symptoms are ignored or treatment is not done right. Pockets grow between the gums and teeth causing the bacteria in plaque to spread below the gum line, resulting in periodontitis. Eventually, these pockets get deeper, which could result in bone loss around the impacted teeth. Receding gums, halitosis (chronic bad breath), and tooth movement are signs of periodontitis. Also, Periodontal disease is closely related to diabetes. Gum disease can make it difficult to manage blood sugar levels, and uncontrolled diabetes can, in turn, worsen gum disease.
Stage 3: Advanced Periodontitis
The worst form of gum disease is advanced periodontitis, which causes substantial bone and tissue loss. Gums peel away from the teeth because of the infection, which leads to abscesses, loose teeth, and chewing difficulties. The Bone and gum health at this stage can only be regained with surgery, particularly bone grafts or flap surgery. Treatment must begin immediately to avoid more issues, such as tooth loss.
Symptoms of Gum Disease
Gum disease symptoms might differ based on the stage, but common symptoms include:
Bleeding, swollen, or red gums.
Halitosis, or chronic foul breath.
Tooth sensitivity.
Chewing pain.
Gum recession.
Loose or moving teeth.
Consult a if you have any of these symptoms and take proper gum disease treatment to avoid causing more harm to your teeth and gums.
How to Prevent Gum Disease
Prevention is better than cure! Here are a few easy routines that will help safeguard your gums.
Use fluoride toothpaste to brush your teeth daily day and night to avoid plaque.
Floss every day to get rid of food particles and plaque that your toothbrush could miss between teeth.
Schedule routine dental checkups to maintain oral hygiene with regular teeth cleaning.
An important risk factor for gum disease is smoking, therefore have a balanced diet and abstain from tobacco products.
Treatment Options for Gum Disease
For Gingivitis:
Gum disease treatment depends on its severity and stage.
Professional scaling and polishing to get rid of tartar and plaque.
Improved brushing and flossing practices as part of an at-home oral hygiene routine.
For Periodontitis:
Deep cleaning, which includes scaling and root planing, for the removal of tartar and plaque below the gum line.
Antibiotics or antimicrobial treatment options to combat infection and lower gum bacteria.
For Advanced Periodontitis:
Surgical methods for restoring bone and gum health, such as bone grafts or flap surgery.
Laser treatments for less painful ways to target and eliminate diseased tissue.

What is Root Canal Therapy?
Root canal treatment is a dental practice of removing the pulp from an infected tooth which is then cleaned, disinfected, filled, and sealed. The treatment helps to remove bacterial infection from the root canal and prevent reinfection of the tooth. You can save your natural tooth with root canal treatment which enables you to chew efficiently and gives you normal biting force and sensation. It also gives your tooth a natural appearance and helps to avoid excessive strain on other teeth.
The root canal is not as painful as you think as you are given anesthesia before the treatment. But it can cause some discomfort for a few days. However, the numbness will be gone within 2-4 hours and it is advised not to eat until it is fully gone.
Signs and Symptoms you might need a Root Canal Treatment
The signs and symptoms of a root canal are:
Persistent tooth pain
- Constant and recurring tooth pain may be an indication of infection in the pulp.
Sensitivity to hot and cold
- Long-lasting sensitivity even after the use of hot or cold substances may be a sign of nerve damage.
Tooth discoloration
- A darkened or grayish tooth can be a sign of pulp damage.
Swollen or tender gums
- Swelling near a painful tooth may indicate the infection spreading to the gum.
Pain when chewing or touching the tooth
- Increased pain can be a sign of a pulp infection.
A cracked or chipped tooth
- This can expose the pulp to bacteria, increasing the risk of infection.
Recurring pimples on the gums (Abscess)
- This small, recurring pimple may indicate a serious infection causing pus buildup.
When to See Your Dentist?
The above-mentioned symptoms are signs indicating the need to consult a
. If you notice your tooth is sensitive to hot or cold sensations, you need a root canal. Ignoring these early signs of sensitivity can lead to severe complications. These signs may indicate an infection in the tooth pulp. Delaying treatment can allow infection to spread, resulting in more complex procedures or even tooth loss. Consulting an endodontist at an early stage can prevent further damage and save the tooth.
What Happens during a Root Canal?
Root canal treatment steps include:
The doctor gives you an anesthesia injection to numb your infected tooth and nearby gums.
Then they apply a dental dam over your infected tooth and nearby gums.
The doctor will drill a small hole in your tooth and use dental instruments to remove the pulp from the tooth.
After removing the pulp they clean and disinfect your pulp chamber and root canal.
Then the doctor fills the pulp chamber and root canals with a thermoplastic material called gutta-percha.
Afterwards they fill the tooth with a temporary dental filling.
Finally the doctor places a dental crown on your treated tooth.
What Happens if you Delay Treatment?
If you pretend not to see the reason for a root canal, it can have an adverse effect on your oral health. It can worsen infection resulting in abscess formation around the tooth root. Over time, this can destroy the surrounding bone, increase pain, and ultimately cause tooth loss. This can worsen the situation and make treatment costly.
Also, an untreated dental infection can spread to other parts of the body, affecting the heart, lungs, and other vital systems. Early treatment can prevent these health risks.
How much does a Root Canal Cost?
The cost of root canal treatment depends on various factors such as the complexity of the problem and the tooth that is affected (whether it is a front tooth, premolar, or molar). As molars are difficult to treat, the cost is comparatively high. This cost may increase if you need additional treatments like a crown after the root canal.
